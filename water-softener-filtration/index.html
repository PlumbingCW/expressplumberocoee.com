Spots, scale and taste

White spots on the glass and a pool smell from the tap

White film dries on the shower door. A crust builds inside the faucet aerator. The kitchen tap smells faintly like a swimming pool. Those are three different complaints, and each one has a different fix.

A water softener is a tank of resin beads that swaps the calcium and magnesium in your water for sodium or potassium, which is what prevents scale. Filtration is a separate job. A carbon filter reduces chlorine taste and odor, and a reverse osmosis unit polishes drinking water at one tap.

For a home in Ocoee, FL the useful question is which complaint you actually have. Match the equipment to that complaint before you spend money on a system that solves a different problem. Ask whether a softener is certified to NSF/ANSI 44, the national standard for residential cation exchange softeners, because the label tells you what the unit was tested to do.

Softener, carbon filter or reverse osmosis: what each one solves

Each device handles one job well and the others poorly. Mixing them up is the most common way to overspend.

Whole-home softener

  • Solves scale on faucets, shower glass and fixtures, and the spotting on dishes.
  • Needs salt or potassium refills, a drain for its regeneration cycle and a power outlet.
  • Does not remove chlorine taste or most other dissolved substances.
  • Adds a little sodium, since sodium replaces the calcium and magnesium.
  • Has a control valve, a brine tank and resin that eventually need service.

Carbon filter and reverse osmosis

  • A carbon filter reduces chlorine taste and odor, at the whole house or at one tap.
  • Cartridges and media must be replaced on a schedule or they stop working.
  • A reverse osmosis unit at the kitchen sink reduces dissolved minerals in drinking and cooking water.
  • Reverse osmosis sends some water to the drain and needs a small tank under the sink.
  • Neither one stops scale on the shower glass or in the water heater.

Some homes pair a whole-home softener with a reverse osmosis tap in the kitchen. Many homes need only one piece.

Our honest take: 5 to 6 grains is moderate, and many homes do fine without a softener

At 5 to 6 grains per gallon, the city's own scale puts Ocoee water at the low end of moderate. Plenty of households manage with a squeegee, vinegar wipes and an occasional aerator cleaning.

A whole-home softener is worth pricing when scale keeps returning after cleaning, when spotting on glass and dishes bothers you every day, or when you own a tankless heater and want less scale reaching its heat exchanger. A tankless water heater still needs flushing on the maker's schedule, softener or not.

It is probably not worth it if taste is the only complaint, because a carbon filter handles chlorine. It is also the wrong tool if you only want better drinking water, since a reverse osmosis tap does that at one sink.

How a treatment setup is planned

A good setup starts with the water and the complaint, not with the equipment.

Name the complaint

Tell the dispatcher what you see and where, such as spots on glass, a slow aerator or taste. Give the brand and model if equipment is already installed or already bought.

Test your tap

A hardness test strip or a lab test on your own tap confirms the city figure where you live. That number sets the capacity of any softener.

Plan the connections

A softener needs inlet and outlet pipes, a bypass valve, a drain with an air gap and an outlet. These are ordinary plumbing installation tasks, and the plumber checks that your tank and valve fit.

Set, flush and check

The valve is programmed for your hardness, the resin bed is flushed and every connection is checked for leaks before the technician leaves.

What changes the price of water treatment

Treatment jobs range from a cartridge swap to a whole-home install, so the factors matter more than any average.

Equipment type and sizeA softener is sized to household water use and measured hardness. A bigger home with more bathrooms needs more capacity than a small one.
Location and plumbing changesA garage or utility closet with a nearby drain and outlet is simple. A long pipe run or a missing drain adds labor.
Filtration stagesCarbon, sediment and reverse osmosis stages each add parts and installation time. A drinking tap needs a faucet hole and a drain connection.
Repair or replaceA failed valve, a cut regeneration line or old resin can often be repaired. A tank that is cracked or very old usually is not worth repairing.
Permit questionsOcoee's published pages do not say how they treat water treatment equipment, so ask the Building Division at 407.905.3104 before work begins.

Homeowners insurance usually responds to sudden and accidental damage, such as a softener line that bursts and floods a room. It usually does not cover wear, a slow leak or the equipment itself. Ask your insurer.
