Sewage backing up into the house: do this first

Sewage in a tub, shower or floor drain is an emergency, and our 24/7 line is open for it. While you wait for a plumber, stop every drop of water going down the drains and keep people out of the wet area.

Each flush or rinse sends more water toward a pipe that is already full, so the mess grows by the minute.

Stop adding water

No showers, no laundry, no dishwasher, no flushing. Even a rinsed pan puts water into the line that is already backed up.

Keep kids and pets away

Backed-up sewage carries bacteria. Wear rubber gloves and boots if you must cross it, and wash your hands well afterward.

Skip the chemical opener

EPA advises against chemical drain openers for a clogged drain. They sit in standing sewage, and the next person to work on the pipe has to handle that liquid.

Note which drains are affected

One slow sink points to a branch clog. A gurgling toilet plus a backed-up tub on the ground floor points to the main line or the lateral.

Photograph the wet area before anyone cleans it, because your insurer may ask. Leave the outdoor cleanout cap closed unless the technician asks you to open it, since a full line will spill onto the yard.

If sewage is coming up out of a street manhole instead of your house, that is the public system. Use the City of Ocoee Report a Problem page at ocoee.org/737, or call City Hall at 407-905-3100 during business hours.

Know the pipe

Your lateral versus the city main

Sewer line repair means fixing the pipe that carries waste from your house to the public sewer, either by replacing the damaged section or by lining it from the inside. That pipe is the lateral. The city main is the larger public pipe it connects to, usually under the street.

Ocoee's sewer use manual defines the lateral as the service line from the public sewer to a point five feet outside the building wall. It does not say plainly who repairs which stretch. Before anyone digs, call City of Ocoee Utilities at 407-905-3100 and ask which side of the connection your problem is on.

Signs the problem is the line and not one drain

Several fixtures act up at once. A toilet gurgles when the washer drains. The lowest tub or floor drain backs up first. Clogs come back within weeks of being cleared, which tells us the blockage keeps re-forming from a damaged section.

Outside, a soggy strip of lawn, a sunken line in the grass or a sewer smell near the cleanout can also mark a broken pipe. A camera inspection of the sewer line confirms it before anyone digs.

Dig and replace, or fix it without a trench

There are three common ways to fix a failed lateral. The right one depends on what the camera shows, not on what is cheapest to advertise.

Open trench replacement

  • Works on any failure: collapsed pipe, offset joints, a low spot holding water.
  • Gives you all-new pipe set to a checked slope.
  • Tears up whatever sits on top: lawn, walkway, driveway or slab.
  • Needs a utility locate before digging and usually a permit.

Trenchless lining or pipe bursting

  • Lining coats the inside of a cleaned pipe with resin. It needs a pipe that is still round and in line.
  • Lining cannot fix a belly where water pools, or a pipe that has shifted apart.
  • Pipe bursting pulls new pipe along the old path and needs a pit at each end.
  • Both mean far less digging, but neither suits every pipe.

A technician decides after cleaning the line and running the camera. If roots have only entered one joint, a short spot repair may be enough. If the pipe is crushed along most of its run, lining is off the table and we will say so. Ask to see the footage that supports whichever method is proposed.

What drives the cost of a sewer line repair

Sewer work ranges from a short spot repair to a full lateral replacement, so there is no honest single number. These are the factors that move the price.

How much pipe is damagedOne cracked joint costs far less to fix than a long run of root-damaged or collapsed pipe.
Depth and accessPipe under a driveway, a tree or the slab is harder to reach than pipe under open lawn.
Repair methodOpen trench, lining and bursting each need different labor, materials and equipment.
Pipe material and sizeOlder clay or cast iron and newer PVC fail in different ways and need different parts.
Permit, locating and restorationPlumbing permits, utility locates and putting the surface back all belong in the total.

Homeowners policies usually respond to sudden and accidental damage, and often not to wear, slow root damage or the repair of the pipe itself. Some policies add a separate sewer or water-backup endorsement. Ask your insurer before work starts, and keep your photos and the written findings.

What Ocoee homeowners should know about their sewer line

Ocoee runs its own wastewater utility, and a few local facts change how a repair is planned.

A city system with a private lateral

City of Ocoee Utilities provides wastewater service. We could not find a published rule on who owns the lateral, so ask the utility which side your failure is on. That answer decides whether you hire a plumber or file a report.

Grease is the city's own warning

The City says grease poured down drains can clog pipes and cause sewer backups. A grease-coated pipe needs cleaning, not a dig, so the camera check comes before any repair quote.

Roots and soil

UF/IFAS notes that roots grow well wherever moisture and nutrients are available, and a leaking joint supplies both. Many Florida upland soils are sandy and drain fast, but we do not assume yours. The camera shows what is actually inside the pipe.

Lining has a state code rule

The Florida Building Commission's declaratory statement DS 2024-044 says lining must follow ASTM F1216 or F1743. Lining only part of a pipe needs building official approval. Ask any lining contractor which standard applies to your job.

Sunken ground over the pipe

The Florida Geological Survey lists broken sewer and drain pipes among causes of ground sinking that can look like a sinkhole. Keep water out of any hole and have the line checked before anyone concludes it is natural.

Plumbing work in Ocoee needs a permit through permits.ocoee.org, and the Building Division is at 407-905-3104. We found no sewer-specific rule, so confirm your scope with them. Our dispatch also reaches nearby Apopka, where the same repair options apply.
