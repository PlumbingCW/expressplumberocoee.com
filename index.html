The first five minutes of a plumbing emergency

Do these in order while you wait for a plumber to call back.

  1. Shut off the main water valve where the supply line enters the house.
  2. Stop using drains and toilets if water is backing up.
  3. Keep power off in any wet area you cannot safely reach.
  4. Call the 24/7 line and tell the plumber where the water is coming from.

What we fix

Plumbing services for Ocoee homes and businesses

Every call starts with the same question: where is the water, and is it still moving? From there the work falls into six groups, and each group links to a full page that explains how we handle it.

Water heaters

Hot water trouble usually starts with a leak, a cold shower or a rumbling tank. Water heater repair comes first when the tank is sound. When it is not, replacement or a planned installation follows, and a tankless unit is worth pricing if you run out of hot water often.

Drains and sewer

A single slow sink is a job for drain cleaning. Repeat clogs call for hydro jetting, and a house-wide backup points to sewer line cleaning. A camera inspection shows what the line looks like, and sewer line repair covers the break itself.

Leaks and pipes

Water where it should not be starts with leak detection, and a floor that feels warm may mean a slab leak. A split line needs burst pipe repair now, and tired, leaking supply lines are the case for repiping.

Fixtures

Most fixture calls are small until a supply line lets go. We handle toilet repair, faucet repair and garbage disposal repair, and we say plainly when a repair costs more than the part is worth.

Installs and water quality

New work gets its own scope under plumbing installation, from vents and rough-ins to gas lines. If spots and scale bother you, water softener and filtration depends on the numbers in the city report, which we walk through with you.

Commercial

Small offices, shops, restaurants and managed buildings get commercial plumbing service with after-hours dispatch, because a leak in a closed building does not wait for Monday.

Why an emergency plumber matters here

Ocoee plumbing, shaped by Ocoee

The facts below come from city reports, state agencies and the National Weather Service. Each one changes how we approach a call, and we explain the link in plain words.

Water from the Floridan Aquifer

The City of Ocoee draws its water from wells between 340 and 1,450 feet deep, then aerates and chlorinates it. The 2025 city report lists hardness at 5 to 6 grains per gallon, which the report itself calls moderate. That means scale builds slowly, so we look at your heater and fixtures before suggesting any treatment.

Purple pipes and reclaimed water

Ocoee sends more than 4 million gallons of reclaimed water a day to irrigation systems. It is treated wastewater, not drinking water, and the city says cross-connections with potable lines are not allowed. If a hose bib or sprinkler line looks wrong, call the city utilities line at 407-905-3159 before touching it.

Storms and saturated ground

Milton in October 2024 dropped 4 to 8 inches of rain across Orange County, and Ian in 2022 produced 10 to 20 inches across central Florida according to the National Hurricane Center. Know where your main shutoff valve is before the season starts, because a pipe that fails in a storm will not wait for a plumber to arrive.

Dips in the yard that look like sinkholes

The Florida Geological Survey says collapsed or broken sewer and drain pipes can mimic sinkhole activity. If you see a new depression near the house, keep water out of it and book a sewer camera inspection so you know whether it is a pipe or something deeper.

Homes from the 1980s and 1990s

A city housing study based on 2000 Census counts found that about seven in ten Ocoee homes were built in 1980 or later, and nearly half in the 1990s. Many of those homes are now on their second or third water heater and may have aging supply lines, so a leak check is a sensible first step when a bill jumps.

Fifty West and the SR 50 corridor

The Fifty West district runs along State Road 50 and is anchored by West Oaks Mall and Orlando Health Central Hospital. Food service tenants there fall under the city sewer manual, which calls for an approved grease interceptor, so commercial plumbing calls often start with grease and drains.
