Cost of Replacing a Water Heater With Tankless in Ocoee, FL

Swapping a tank for a tankless heater costs more than replacing it with another tank. Here is what the published ranges say, and what raises or lowers the bill in an Ocoee home.

What the numbers say

A national range, and why your Ocoee quote may land outside it

NerdWallet's national cost guide, updated in September 2025, puts a tank-style heater at $600 to $2,500 and a tankless heater at about $1,400 to $3,900, installation included. Those are not Florida or Ocoee figures, and we found no published Orange County range to use instead.

Replacing a tank with tankless usually costs more than replacing it with another tank, because the job takes extra labor. NerdWallet notes that labor is more than half of the total in many installs.

So treat the range as a sanity check, not a quote. Ocoee Express Plumbing gives a clear price before work starts, after a plumber has seen your setup. We do not quote a tankless swap over the phone, because the gas, vent and electrical details decide it.

What moves the price up or down

Two houses with the same heater model can get very different totals. These are the choices that set the base price before any site problems show up.

Fuel and conversionReplacing gas with gas is the simplest path. Switching from electric to gas, or the reverse, adds new supply work and raises labor.
Unit size and flowA heater built for a bigger household or more fixtures at once costs more and draws more fuel or power.
Where it mountsA wall in an open garage is quick. A tight closet, an attic or a spot far from the old tank means more pipe and more time.
Labor hoursBecause labor is the biggest share of a national average, a tricky install moves the total more than the heater does.
Add-ons the plumber recommendsExtra valves, fittings or code items change the total. Ask for each one to be listed by name and price.

Homeowners insurance usually responds to sudden and accidental damage, such as a burst line, and not to wear, a gradual leak or the heater itself. A planned upgrade is not an insured event. Ask your insurer how your own policy treats a failed heater.

Tankless or another tank: what you pay for

The cheaper heater is not always the cheaper choice over time, and the reverse is also true.

Another tank-style heater

  • Lower national price range, $600 to $2,500
  • Fits the existing gas, vent and electric setup more often
  • ENERGY STAR says a replacement made in an emergency can limit you to less efficient models

A tankless heater

  • Higher national price range, about $1,400 to $3,900
  • ENERGY STAR gives gas tankless a life expectancy of 20 years
  • ENERGY STAR says a certified gas tankless saves a family of four about $95 a year on gas bills against a standard gas tank

That $95 is a national figure with a gas tank as the baseline, so an electric home will see different savings. A saving that size takes years to offset a higher install cost, which is why the 20-year life matters to the math.

Rebates and credits as of today

The federal 25C home improvement credit does not apply to a heater placed in service after December 31, 2025, per the IRS. If a quote you see still counts on that credit, ask what it assumes.

Duke Energy Florida's water heater rebate covers heat pump units only and states it is not available on tankless heaters. Peoples Gas lists $700 for a new gas tankless replacing an electric unit and $550 replacing a gas unit, but only for its customers, and it reserves the right to change or end the offer.

We could not confirm which gas or electric company serves a given Ocoee address. Check your bill, read the current terms on the utility's own page, and ask before the install, not after.
