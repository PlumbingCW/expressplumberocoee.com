The first ten minutes: water off, power safe, belongings up

Water is moving faster than you are, so act first and call second. Our 24/7 line is open, and these are the steps to take while you wait.

Close the house main

The valve is usually where the supply line enters the house. Turn it fully, then open the lowest faucet to drain the pipes. If it will not turn, say so on the call.

Make the power safe

If water is near outlets, appliances or the panel, do not wade in. Switch off breakers only from dry floor. Otherwise leave it to dispatch.

Lift what water will ruin

Move rugs, electronics and cardboard up, and raise furniture onto blocks. Use a wet vacuum only if it is rated for water.

Take photos before you mop

Shoot the break, the wet walls and the damaged items. With the main off and lines drained, switch the water heater off too.

Night and weekend calls reach the same dispatch line. After-hours visits are for water still flowing or reaching finished rooms. A contained break with the main closed can often move to a same-day appointment when available.

What happens when the plumber arrives

You do not need to stand at the break to follow the repair. This is the usual order of work.

Confirm the water is off and find the source

The plumber checks that your shutoff holds, opens the area around the break and traces the pipe back to dry, sound material.

Isolate the damaged run

Valves or temporary caps let the rest of the house keep water while the broken section is out of service.

Cut out and replace the damaged section

A burst section is cut back to solid pipe, and new pipe and the right fittings go in. Matching the material correctly matters more than speed.

Pressure test and review

The line is tested before anything is closed up. The plumber says whether the rest of the pipe looks likely to fail. Drying and wall repair are other trades.

Water still flowing?

Close the house main, then call. Tell dispatch what is spraying and whether the shutoff held.

Broken pipe, failed supply valve or service line: telling them apart

Where the water appears tells you which problem you have, and it decides who needs to be called.

Spray at a sink or toilet

A failed supply valve or stop shows as water at one fixture. Closing the stop under the fixture may end it. If the valve will not close, shut the main.

Water through a wall or ceiling

A split pipe inside the structure. Shut the main and expect an opening in drywall to reach it.

Soggy ground between meter and house

The line from the meter to the house is the likely culprit. A wet patch, bubbling water or a sunken strip in the yard all point there.

Water at the water heater

Connectors and supply pipes at a tank in a garage or attic can fail. A leaking tank itself is a water heater job. If the meter also runs with taps closed, see our leak detection page.

The repair depends on what the pipe is made of, so tell dispatch what you can see. We cannot identify a pipe material over the phone.

The line between the meter and your house: who handles which side

The City of Ocoee's service form says the city maintains the meter and the delivery system up to and including the meter. The customer maintains the delivery system and fixtures from the meter to and including the house. A burst on your side is your repair. A break at or before the meter belongs to the city.

Which side is the break on?

Close your house shutoff and watch the meter, which the EPA says is usually underground near the curb. If it stops, the break is inside. If it keeps moving, the break is on the buried line you own.

For the meter itself, call Utilities at 407-905-3159. The city lists 407-428-5766 as its after-hours emergency line, but its page does not say that number is for main breaks.

Before anyone digs

Florida law requires a Sunshine 811 call at least two full business days before digging. Replacing a long run rearranges pipes, so ask the Building Division whether new pipe work needs a permit. The city's 2024 lead inventory found no lead or galvanized service lines needing replacement. That covers the city side, not your house piping.

Storms, cold nights and water pressure in Ocoee

Three local pressures stress pipes here.

Hurricanes and fallen trees

NWS Melbourne's Hurricane Charley report notes widespread house damage mostly from fallen trees. Its Milton narrative cites 4 to 8 inches of rain and street flooding in Orange County. No report we found counts burst pipes in Ocoee, so after a storm, check the meter with all water off and walk the yard for soft ground.

Cold snaps are rare but real

NWS Melbourne documented record-cold days in January 2010. Freezes are infrequent here, so attic pipes and outdoor spigots are usually unprotected. Before a forecast freeze, cover spigots and find your shutoff.

Heated water expands

The Florida Building Code requires thermal expansion control where a storage water heater's cold supply passes through a check valve, pressure reducing valve or backflow preventer. We cannot say which Ocoee homes have one. If a relief valve drips or pipes bang, have it checked, since pressure spikes stress joints.

Purple pipe is not drinking water

Ocoee's reclaimed water ordinance describes reclaim pipe and meter boxes as purple. A broken purple pipe in a yard is irrigation. Call Utilities at 407-905-3159 and never connect it to house plumbing.
