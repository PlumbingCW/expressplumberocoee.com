Hard water in Apopka and what scale does to heaters and fixtures

The City of Apopka's 2025 water quality report calls its water moderately hard. Recent tests ran from 120 mg/L to 200 mg/L, which is 7.0 to 11.7 grains per gallon. Water in that range leaves mineral scale on heating elements, tank bottoms, showerheads and faucet aerators.

Scale makes a water heater work harder and can shorten the time before it needs service. A tank that rumbles or pops as it heats is a common sign, and water heater repair usually begins with flushing and testing it. Your own tap may sit above or below the city range, so a home test kit tells you more.

Tankless units are sensitive to scale because water passes through narrow channels. In hard water they are normally descaled on a schedule, and the maker's manual sets the interval. If your tankless water heater throws errors or runs cold, scale is one of the first things to check.

Reclaimed irrigation: a second water system in your yard

Apopka's reclaimed program delivers about 8 million gallons a day to over 9,300 homes and businesses, and the city has run reuse for almost 50 years. Reclaimed water is treated wastewater meant for irrigation, not for drinking. If your sprinklers run on it, never tie that line into the house plumbing.

The city also runs a cross-connection program, which exists to keep irrigation and drinking water apart. A plumber doing any work near the meter or the irrigation tie-in should ask which water your yard uses. If you are not sure, call the city utility on 407-703-1731 before you touch the lines.

Grease, restaurant drains and the city program

Apopka runs a City Oil and Grease Management Program under Ordinance 3152. It has separate pages for residential and non-residential properties, and certificates go through the city's OpenGov portal. Restaurants and food businesses should read the city's rules and confirm what applies to their kitchen.

Grease is also what clogs most kitchen lines, at home or in a restaurant. Drain cleaning clears the blockage but does not replace the upkeep the city program asks for. A kitchen drain that backs up during service is an emergency, and our 24/7 line is open for it.

Apopka plumbing emergency: what to do before we arrive

Call our 24/7 line, then work through the case that matches yours.

Water flooding from a line

Close the main valve where the supply enters the house and open a low faucet. If the water is reclaimed irrigation water, keep children and pets away from it.

Kitchen drain backing up

Stop the dishwasher and sinks. Do not pour grease or chemical opener down the line, and tell dispatch whether it is a home or a restaurant.

Heater leaking or no hot water

Turn off the heater's power at the breaker and the cold water feed. If you smell gas, leave first and call 911 or the gas utility.
