Sewer Backup in Ocoee, FL: What to Do First

When sewage rises in a tub, shower or floor drain, stop using water, keep people and pets away, and call a plumber. Here is the order that works for Ocoee homes.

What to do in the first few minutes of a sewer backup

Our 24/7 line is open for backups, and same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows. While you wait for a plumber, these four steps keep the problem from growing.

Stop using water

Do not flush, run the dishwasher, start the washing machine or take a shower. Every gallon you send down goes to the lowest open drain in the house, and that is where the sewage is coming up.

Keep people, pets and food away

Sewage is contaminated. Keep children and animals out of the room, avoid touching it with bare hands, and wash up after any contact.

Skip the chemical drain opener

EPA advises avoiding chemical drain openers for a clogged drain. In a backup the liquid can sit in standing sewage or splash back on you.

Note which drains are affected

Tell the dispatcher whether one fixture or several are backing up, and which is lowest. Take photos of the damage for your insurer before you clean anything.

Who to call, and which utility serves you

Ocoee runs its own water and sewer utility, but not every address in the area uses it. Orange County Utilities, known as OCU, serves other homes. Your utility bill tells you which, and that decides who gets the call after the plumber.

Your private line or the main: how to tell which one failed

A plumber settles this, but a few clues help you describe the problem.

Points to your own sewer line

  • Only your house is affected and neighbors are fine
  • The lowest drain in your house backs up first
  • The street looks normal and there is no overflow nearby

Points to the main in the street

  • Neighbors report the same problem
  • Sewage is surfacing at a manhole or in the road
  • More than one home in the block lost drain function at once

What the City of Ocoee says about backups

The city says pouring fats, oils and grease down the drain can clog pipes and cause sewer backups in the community. Its tips are to let grease cool and put it in the trash, scrape plates, and use a sink strainer. If grease was part of your backup, those habits are the cheapest prevention.

Wipes are a separate problem. EPA lists non-flushable and baby wipes among items to never flush, and UF/IFAS says flushable wipes can take longer to break down than toilet paper and may cause major sewer blockages. Ocoee has not published wipe guidance that we could find, so follow EPA's list.

Source type: City of Ocoee Fats, Oils and Grease page, EPA and UF/IFAS

After a storm or power outage

OCU's storm guidance says that without power, pump stations may be down and sewer backups can result. It advises using less water, draining bathtub water slowly, and avoiding heavy water use right after power returns. It is county guidance, and we found no Ocoee equivalent, so ask Ocoee Utilities if you live on the city system.

So after an outage, hold off on laundry and long showers for a while. If a drain starts bubbling or sewage appears, follow the steps at the top and call.
