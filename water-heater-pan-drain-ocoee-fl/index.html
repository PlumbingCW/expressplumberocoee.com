Water Heater Pan and Drain Rules for Ocoee, FL Homes

A tank water heater needs a pan wherever a leak would damage the building. Here is what that means for heaters in Ocoee garages, closets and attics.

The rule in plain terms

When a tank heater needs a pan under it

The Florida Building Code, Plumbing, says a storage tank water heater must sit in a pan where a leak from the tank would cause damage. The pan has an indirect drain pipe so that water has somewhere to go besides your floor. A plastic pan is not allowed under a gas-fired heater.

A replacement heater has a gentler rule. If the old installation never had a pan drain, the code does not make you add one just because the tank is new. The pan requirement itself still follows the damage test, so the installer looks at what the old setup was and what the new one needs.

The pan is there to catch a slow leak, not to fix it. Once water is in the pan, the heater or a fitting has a problem, and the pan only buys you time.

Where your heater sits, and what that means for the pan

The code does not list rooms. It asks whether a leak would cause damage, and the plumber and the building official apply that to your layout. This table shows how that usually plays out.

Where the heater sitsWhat a leak would reachWhat to expect
Attic, above a finished ceilingInsulation, drywall and the rooms below, often before anyone sees waterA pan is the clear case. A slow drip can stain a ceiling long before you notice
Closet inside living spaceFlooring, walls and neighboring roomsA pan is expected, since a leak spreads into finished areas
Garage with a concrete floorBoxes, stored items and the wall baseAsk the installer how the code applies, since damage is harder to define here
Utility room with a floor drainWater may run to the drainThe plumber decides, and the drain may change what is needed
Any gas-fired heaterSame as aboveMetal or approved non-plastic pan only, never plastic

If you are unsure how the code reads for your house, ask your plumber before the heater is set, not after.

What to do if water is in the pan or on the floor

Treat any water under the heater as a live leak until a plumber says otherwise. Start at the cold supply valve above the tank and turn it off. An electric heater also gets its breaker switched off. A gas heater has a control marked for its off setting, so use the label on the unit.

A gas smell changes the plan. Get everyone outside, leave light switches alone, and call the gas utility or 911 from there before anything else.

A relief valve that drips or discharges is a sign for a plumber, not something to cap or plug. The relief valve is the safety device and is not meant to manage pressure from thermal expansion, so a drip points to a fault elsewhere. Reading the water: where it comes from

Not every wet pan means a failed tank. A loose supply fitting, a weeping relief valve or a drip from a connection can each put water in the pan. A plumber follows the water back from the pan to the source, and the answer decides between a repair and replacement.

Rust at the base of the tank or water seeping from a seam is a different story, and usually a replacement job. If you are weighing it, our water heater repair visits look at fittings and valves first, and an old tank that leaks from the body is usually a swap with proper water heater installation and a pan that fits.

Which code edition applies on your date

The 2023 edition of the Florida Building Code is the one in force today. The Ninth Edition takes effect on December 31, 2026, according to a Miami-Dade County notice. Section numbers can shift between editions, so we do not quote them here.

For you, that means a heater installed or permitted after the new year may be judged under the newer book. Ask your plumber which edition applies to your permit date, and do not rely on an old article, ours included, for the exact wording.

Permits for a heater swap in Ocoee

Ocoee's FAQ lists plumbing among the work that needs a building permit, and permit applications go through permits.ocoee.org or the Building Division at City Hall, 1 N. Bluford Ave. The FAQ does not mention water heaters by name, and we could not confirm a water-heater-specific rule.

So call the Building Division at 407-905-3100 and ask how your swap is handled before the old tank comes out. Ask your plumber who applies for the permit and when the inspector will look at the pan and drain.
