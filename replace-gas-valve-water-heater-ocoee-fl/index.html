How to Replace a Gas Valve on a Water Heater in Ocoee, FL

Replacing the gas control valve on a water heater is gas work, and in Ocoee we recommend a licensed plumber do it. This guide covers what the valve does, how to tell it has failed, and how to decide between a new valve and a new heater.

If you smell gas, leave first

Do not look for the leak. Get everyone out of the house, and do not flip a light switch or run any appliance, because a spark can ignite gas.

From outside, call your gas utility's emergency line or 911. If Peoples Gas serves you, the number on its safety page is 1-800-400-4271. We could not confirm which gas company serves every Ocoee street, so check your gas bill. Call a plumber only after the gas company says the house is safe.

Should you replace the gas valve yourself?

For most homeowners the answer is no. A gas control valve sits between the gas line and the burner. A swap that leaves a small leak at a fitting can put gas in your utility closet or garage, where a pilot light is ready to ignite it.

Florida law treats gas piping as licensed plumbing work. Under section 489.105(3)(m) of the Florida Statutes, a plumbing contractor's scope includes installing and repairing natural gas and liquefied petroleum gas piping and related venting.

What about the owner-builder exemption?

Section 489.103(7) lets an owner act as their own contractor on a one or two family home they live in and do not offer for sale or lease, with direct onsite supervision of the work. That statute does not say a permit is unnecessary. The building code still requires a permit to replace a gas system or plumbing system.

Signs the gas valve may be failing

These symptoms can point to the valve. They can also point to a worn thermocouple, a blocked vent or sediment in the tank, which is why a technician checks those first.

Pilot will not stay lit

If the flame goes out soon after you relight it, the safety part, a thermocouple, may be bad. If a new one does not fix it, the valve is next.

Burner never ignites

The thermostat calls for heat but nothing lights. The valve may not be opening, or the igniter on a newer unit may have failed.

Water too hot or too cold

The temperature dial is part of the valve. A dial that no longer changes the water temperature points to the control inside.

Dial is stuck or loose

A knob that will not turn, or turns freely without clicking, is worn. Do not force it. Gas control parts are not meant to be pried.

Gas smell near the unit

That is an emergency, not a diagnostic clue. Use the steps in the box above.

Replace the valve or replace the water heater

A new valve only makes sense when the rest of the heater is sound. These decision rules help sort the two paths.

Valve swap makes sense when

  • The tank is dry, with no rust streaks or water at the base
  • The heater is relatively young, since ENERGY STAR suggests considering a replacement for a unit older than 10 years
  • The maker still supplies a matching valve for that model
  • A technician finds the valve is the only fault

Replace the heater when

  • The tank leaks, which a new valve cannot fix
  • You see corrosion, rust in the water, or rumbling from sediment, all signs ENERGY STAR lists
  • The heater is past 10 years and a sudden failure would leave you without hot water
  • A repair would put a new part into a tank near the end of its life

What you can safely do while you wait

If you do not smell gas but the heater is not working, you can turn the gas shutoff on the pipe that feeds it a quarter turn so the handle sits across the pipe. Leave the valve itself and the burner alone. Taking the heater's cover or door off is also a job for a technician.

Check that nothing is stored against the heater and that the area is dry. The code text also says a plastic pan must not sit beneath a gas-fired water heater, so mention it if you see one.
