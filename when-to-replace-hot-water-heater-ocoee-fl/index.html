When to Replace a Hot Water Heater in Ocoee, FL

A tank heater older than 10 years, or any heater that leaks or rusts, is a replacement candidate. Here is how Ocoee homeowners can tell a repair from a swap.

The short answer

Ten years old plus one warning sign means start planning

ENERGY STAR tells homeowners to consider replacing a water heater once it is more than 10 years old, before a failure forces the choice. For an Ocoee home, that is the line to use. A heater under 10 that works fine can stay. One over 10 with any sign in the table below should be on your replacement list now.

You can check the age yourself. The label on the tank carries the brand and a serial number, and manufacturers publish how to read the serial. ENERGY STAR suggests exactly that check. If the label is gone or unreadable, assume the heater is older than you hope.

Planning matters because failure rarely picks a good moment. ENERGY STAR warns that a sudden breakdown pushes people into a rushed purchase that can cost more and limit them to less efficient models.

What you notice, and whether to wait or replace

The signs below come from ENERGY STAR's list of warning signs. How much each one matters depends on the age of the tank.

Four checks you can do from the floor

None of these need a ladder or a tool, and none involve opening the heater.

Read the label

Write down the brand and serial number. Decoding the serial gives you the build date, which is the one fact that settles the age question.

Look under the tank

Check the floor or pan for damp spots, drips or stains. Do it after the heater has run a heating cycle.

Run the hot tap into a white cup

Brown or rusty water from hot taps only suggests the tank. Rust from both hot and cold suggests the pipes.

Listen after a heating cycle

Rumbling or popping while it heats points to sediment on the tank bottom. Note how long it has been happening.

Repair it or replace it

Age and the type of fault decide this, not a single rule.

Repair tends to make sense when

  • The heater is under 10 years old
  • The fault is a part, such as a valve, a thermostat or an element
  • The tank itself is dry and clean outside
  • Hot water supply is otherwise steady

Replacement tends to make sense when

  • The heater is past 10 years old
  • Water is leaking from the tank body or seams
  • There is visible corrosion on the tank
  • Repairs are repeating, or rust is coming out of the hot taps

A plumber can tell you which side you are on after looking at the tank. We do not diagnose a heater over the phone, and we will not tell you a working young heater needs replacing.

Ocoee's water report and your heater

The City of Ocoee's 2025 drinking water quality report lists hardness at 5 to 6 grains per gallon. The city's own scale calls 5 to 12 moderate. ENERGY STAR says minerals in water can contribute to tank corrosion in general, but we found no source showing Ocoee water shortens heater life.

So do not replace a healthy heater early because of the water. Judge it by age and condition. What a replacement involves under Florida code

The Florida Building Code, Plumbing, says a tank heater must sit in a pan where a leak would damage the building. A plastic pan is not allowed under a gas-fired heater. A replacement does not have to add a pan drain if the old setup never had one, so your plumber looks at what is already there.

The code also calls for a thermal expansion control device on the cold supply when that supply passes through a check valve, a pressure reducing valve or a backflow preventer. We cannot tell from here whether your house has one of those devices, so the plumber checks the supply side when planning the job. The relief valve is not meant to handle expansion.

Replacing a heater is plumbing work, and the building code requires a permit to replace a plumbing or gas system. We could not find an Ocoee page that spells out a rule for water heaters in particular, so ask the Building Division about your address or look at permits.ocoee.org. If the heater fails before you are ready

Close the cold water valve on the pipe feeding the heater, then switch the electric breaker off. For a gas unit, follow the control label to turn the gas control down. If you smell gas, leave the house, do not touch switches, and call your gas utility or 911 from outside before you call anyone else.
