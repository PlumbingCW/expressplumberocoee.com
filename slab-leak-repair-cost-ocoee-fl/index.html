What three national guides put on a slab leak

A slab leak is a break in a water line that runs under or inside the concrete floor of your home. Three national cost guides average the repair at about $2,280 to $2,500. None of them is based on Ocoee or Florida jobs, and we found no published Orange County figure.

An average hides the real story. The same guides list a typical range from about $630 to $4,400, and two of them list rerouting as high as $15,000. What you pay depends on how the plumber reaches the pipe, so ask which method is quoted before you compare any two prices.

What sets the price of a slab leak repair

Four or five choices explain most of the gap between a small bill and a large one.

How the pipe is reachedA spot repair through a small opening costs far less than tunneling under the slab or opening a large area of floor.
Length and material of pipeA short section is simple. If the pipe is old or failing in several places, repeat leaks make a longer replacement more sensible.
Finding the leak firstDetection is its own line in every guide. A precise location keeps the opening small, so it can lower the repair cost.
Floor and finish restorationTile, wood or carpet over the leak must be lifted and put back. This can add a lot and is often a separate trade.
Rerouting instead of repairingRunning new line through walls or the attic avoids the slab but means more pipe and more labor. It drives the high end of every range.

Homeowners policies typically respond to sudden and accidental damage and not to wear, a gradual leak or the pipe itself. Some may cover the water damage and tear-out that follow a covered leak. Ask your insurer before work begins, and keep photos and receipts.

Permits, ground and the sinkhole question

Ocoee's own FAQ lists plumbing among the work that needs a building permit, applied for at permits.ocoee.org or the Building Division at City Hall. We found no rule written specifically for slab leaks, so confirm your scope with the Building Division at 407-905-3100 before a reroute. Also ask who pulls the permit and where its fee sits on your quote.

The Florida Geological Survey says the whole state sits on carbonate rock, so sinkholes could theoretically form anywhere. It also lists broken sewer and drain pipes among things that mimic sinkhole activity. If the ground settles near your slab, tell the plumber, because a broken drain line is as likely a culprit as the supply line. FGS advises against pouring water into a hole, and its Sinkhole Helpline is 850-245-2118.

Before you pay for a repair, confirm it is a slab leak

Many suspected slab leaks turn out to be a toilet, an irrigation line or a fixture. These steps cost nothing.

Run the two-hour meter test

EPA says to read your water meter with no water in use, then read it again two hours later. If it changed, you probably have a leak. The meter is usually underground near the curb.

Check the toilets

EPA suggests a few drops of food coloring in the tank. If color reaches the bowl within 10 minutes, the flapper is leaking.

Look at your usage online

Ocoee's Customer Portal shows usage in near real time and can alert you to a suspected leak. The utility help line is 407-905-3159.

Know where the city's job ends

The city's service form puts maintenance from the meter to the house on the customer. A leak after the meter is yours to repair.
