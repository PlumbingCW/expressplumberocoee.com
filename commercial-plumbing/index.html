Who calls us

Who we work with: owners, managers and tenants of Ocoee buildings

If water is flooding your building or a drain is overflowing, our 24/7 line is open for commercial calls. While you wait, close the main shutoff, keep people and electronics away from the water, and tell anyone sharing the line that water is off.

Commercial plumbing is service on the water supply, drains and fixtures of a business or multi-unit property, where the caller often does not own the pipes. We work with small offices, retail shops, restaurants, multifamily communities and the property managers who run them.

Much of Ocoee's commercial activity sits along SR 50. The city's Fifty West district runs along it between SR 429 and Clarke Road, and includes the West Oaks Mall area. Where several tenants share one building, one water supply or one sewer line can serve all of them, so learn where your shutoffs and cleanouts are before the night you need them.

Water you cannot see: wet pavement, stained ceilings, odd bills

A damp patch in an apartment parking lot, a stain spreading across a ceiling, or a bill that jumps with no change in use all point to a leak not yet found. Leak detection narrows the location so the repair opens as little pavement or ceiling as possible, by listening, pressure testing or tracing, depending on the pipe.

If your account is with Ocoee Utilities, the city's customer portal shows usage in near real time and can alert you to a suspected leak. Help is at 407-905-3159.

Shut off the water first, then tell tenants and document the damage

After hours, the order matters. Stopping the water comes first, and what you do next shapes both the repair and any claim.

Shut off the water

Close the building main or the zone valve for the affected unit. If you do not know where it is, the owner or property manager should. Do not open walls or ceilings.

Make the area safe

Water near outlets, panels and light fixtures is an electrical hazard. Keep people out of standing water, and cut power to a wet room only if the breaker is dry and reachable.

Tell tenants and the owner

Tell the tenants on the affected line why water is off. Call the owner or manager, and have the unit numbers ready so the plumber knows where to go.

Document before cleanup

Photograph the source, the damage and the water meter. Note the time you found it. Keep wet materials until an insurer or adjuster allows removal.

A floor drain that overflows, a restroom that backs up, or gurgling across several fixtures points to the building drain, not one fixture. Stop using water on that line. Sewer line cleaning or drain cleaning clears it, and a stopped lift pump needs repair before the floor drain will keep up.

We take commercial calls same-day when appointments are available, and after hours the dispatch line sends a licensed plumber for active flooding and backups. A slow leak you have stopped at the shutoff can often wait for morning.

How a service call is scheduled for a managed property

Property managers rarely stand at the sink. A call goes better when the facts arrive in one message.

Give the address, area and contact

Tell dispatch the building address, the unit or area, and who will let the plumber in. Share what you see, such as wet carpet, brown water or odor.

Name who approves the work

Say who can approve the work and whether a work order number is needed. The clear price before work starts goes to that person.

Plan access and shutoffs

Confirm where the shutoffs are and whether tenants need notice. Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows. True flooding goes to emergency dispatch.

Close the loop with the owner

Ask for a written note of what was found and changed, with the date, to pass to the owner, the insurer or the tenant. Give billing details at the start.

Reactive call or planned maintenance visit

Most commercial plumbing calls start when water is already on the floor. A planned visit moves part of that work to a calm hour.

Reactive call

  • Starts after water is on the floor
  • Often after hours, with limited access and no history of the building
  • Damage grows while you find the shutoff and the right contact
  • Right for true failures, costly when the warning signs sat for weeks

Planned visit

  • Booked for a time when the space is quiet
  • Shutoffs found, labeled and tested before they are needed
  • Water heaters, floor drains, cleanouts and visible supply lines looked over
  • Backflow and grease interceptor due dates tracked in one place

A planned visit shows what age and wear are doing, but it cannot predict every failure, and pipes behind walls stay hidden. When you book, say which items you want looked at, and we will tell you what we can and cannot do.

Backflow testing and grease trap pumping: usually the owner's job

Backflow assemblies protect the public water supply from a building's plumbing, and some utilities require a certified tester to check them on a schedule. Orange County Utilities' cross-connection guide, written for its own customers, makes the commercial owner responsible. We found no Ocoee statement, so ask Ocoee Utilities at 407-905-3159 whether your account has a test due.

The city's sewer use manual, an undated text, says food businesses must have an approved oil and grease interceptor and prohibits under-sink interceptors for new facilities. Apartment communities found to cause stoppages can be ordered to stop discharging grease or install an interceptor. Confirm the current text with Utilities.

How often, who may do the work and what to file are set by the local utility and the health or building authorities. Pump-out is usually booked with a grease hauler. Keep any utility notice handy when you call us about the lines and fixtures around it.

What affects commercial plumbing prices

A leaking restroom supply and a collapsed building drain are very different jobs. These factors set the number.

Urgency and timingActive flooding at night is dispatched as an emergency. A contained problem can often wait for a same-day appointment when available.
AccessOpen ceilings and exposed pipe are quick. Roofs, finished tenant space and paved areas add work.
ScopeFinding a leak, clearing a line and repairing a pipe are separate jobs, and one visit may involve more than one.
Building size and tenantsMore units and shared lines mean more to isolate, test and coordinate.
Permits and inspectionLarger repairs may need a city permit. Ask the Building Division at 407-905-3104 when in doubt.

Commercial property policies typically respond to sudden and accidental damage, and not to wear, gradual leaks or the failed part itself. Your lease decides who carries what between landlord and tenant. Ask your insurer and read the lease before you commit to work.
