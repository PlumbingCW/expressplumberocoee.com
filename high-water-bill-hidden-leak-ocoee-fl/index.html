High Water Bill in Ocoee, FL: How to Find the Hidden Leak Behind It

A high water bill in Ocoee usually traces to a toilet, a sprinkler zone or a buried line, and you can often tell which one from the bill and the city portal before you call anyone.

Start with the bill, not the pipes

Hidden leaks are often small and steady. A bill that jumps with no change in your habits is the signal, and the pattern of the jump tells you where to look. A toilet that runs all day adds a little to every billing day, while a broken sprinkler line adds a lot on the days the system runs.

Pull the last three bills and compare the gallons used, not the dollar total. If the gallon figure climbed and stayed up, the leak is constant. If it spiked once and dropped, think about a one-time event such as a filled pool, a long sprinkler test or a hose left running.

Four checks that narrow down a hidden leak

Work through these in order. Each one removes a suspect.

Read the daily usage chart

Log in to the city's Sensus Analytics Customer Portal, which shows usage in near real time. Look at the hours when everyone is asleep or out. Usage that stays above zero then means water is escaping.

Check every toilet

Put a few drops of food coloring in each tank, wait ten minutes and look in the bowl without flushing. Color in the bowl means the flapper is leaking.

Run each sprinkler zone by hand

Walk the yard while each zone runs and look for geysers, soggy ground and heads that sink or spray sideways. Turn the controller to its manual setting first.

Test the meter against your main valve

With all use off, watch the meter dial for a short time. Then close the house main valve and watch again. If the meter still moves, the loss is on the pipe between the meter and the house.

Leak source, how it shows on the bill and what fixes it

Matching the bill pattern to the source saves a lot of guessing.

Leak sourceHow it shows upTypical fix
Toilet flapper or fill valveA steady rise every day, night and day alikeReplace the flapper or fill valve
Irrigation line or headExtra use on the days and hours the sprinklers run, wet ground in the yardRepair the broken line or head, then retest the zone
Dripping faucet or shower valveA smaller steady rise, often with a visible dripReplace the washer, cartridge or valve

Ocoee's Leak Detection page names toilet flappers, faucets and showerheads as common leaks and says a leak can waste hundreds of gallons a day.

Signs a leak is outdoors or in the irrigation system

Yard leaks are easy to miss because the water goes into the ground and out of sight.

Green strips or lush patches in dry weather

A leaking line feeds one stretch of lawn better than the rest.

Soft ground near the meter or along the house

Standing wet spots when it has not rained suggest a line below.

Low pressure at hose bibs or sprinkler heads

Water lost upstream reduces what reaches the end of the line.

Irrigation that runs when the ground is already wet

A controller that follows a fixed schedule wastes water in rainy weeks.

What Ocoee tells customers about leaks, rain and irrigation

The City of Ocoee's rate sheet effective October 1, 2026 prices residential water in tiers that step up at 6,000, 12,000, 18,000, 24,000 and 30,000 gallons, and each step costs more per gallon than the one before. A steady leak can push a home into a higher tier, which is why a leak can raise the bill by more than the lost gallons alone suggest. Check the current rate sheet for exact numbers.

The city's Water Conservation page says Ocoee receives about 50 inches of rain a year and recommends no irrigation between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m. Reclaimed water is not under the watering restrictions, but the city still makes that recommendation. If your yard is on reclaimed water, a sprinkler leak will show on that separate account, so look at the reclaimed line item too.

We could not find a published Ocoee policy for adjusting a bill after a leak. Do not assume one exists. Call Utility Billing at 407-905-3191 to ask, and keep your repair paperwork. Ocoee also says the customer maintains the pipes from the meter to the house, so the repair of a leak on your side falls to you.

Source type: City of Ocoee 2026 rate sheet, Water Conservation, Customer Portal and Utility Customer Service Request Form

After a storm or heavy rain week

Storm weeks change water habits and can mask a leak. Rain cuts outdoor use, so a bill that should drop but stays flat is a clue. Fallen limbs and debris can also snap sprinkler lines, so walk the yard once the weather clears and run each zone.
