Epoxy Lining Sewer Pipes in Ocoee, FL: Pros, Cons and Code Limits

Epoxy lining sewer pipes can repair a cracked or worn line in Ocoee without a trench, but it only fits pipe that is still in the right place.

What epoxy lining does and where it stops

Epoxy lining, often called cured-in-place pipe or CIPP, puts a new resin-coated tube inside the old sewer pipe. The tube is pressed against the wall and hardened, so the old pipe becomes a host for a new inner pipe. The ICC's code notes describe it as a resin-saturated felt tube inverted into a cleaned pipe and cured by heat or UV light.

The big benefit is that the yard, driveway and landscaping mostly stay in place. The big limit is that a liner follows the shape of the pipe it goes into. If the old pipe has dropped, shifted or collapsed, the liner will carry that fault with it. A camera inspection decides which case you have, so sewer inspection comes before any lining quote.

Lining versus digging up the line

Both fix a broken sewer line. They suit different damage, and neither is better in every case. If the problem is only a clog, sewer line cleaning comes first.

Epoxy or CIPP lining

  • Works through small access points, so less of the yard and hard surfaces are opened.
  • Suits cracks, joint gaps, root entry and rough pipe walls in a line that still runs straight.
  • Needs a clean pipe first, so cutting out roots and debris is part of the job.
  • Leaves the old pipe in place, so a bad slope or a collapsed section is not corrected.

Open-trench [[sewer-line-repair|sewer line repair]]

  • Replaces the failed pipe with new pipe, which can fix a sag, an offset or a collapse.
  • Opens the ground, so it affects lawns, driveways and anything above the line.
  • Calls for a Sunshine 811 locate before digging, which adds time.
  • May be the only answer when the old pipe has lost its shape.

We cannot say which will cost less for your home without seeing the line, and we do not quote costs here. A clear price comes before work starts.

When lining fits and when it does not

This table reflects how the trade generally describes the choice. We found no Florida government source that lists failure cases for lining, so ask any contractor to show you the camera footage that supports the recommendation.

Hairline cracks or open joints in straight pipeOften yesThe liner seals the wall without moving the pipe

A sagging line often shows up first as repeat clogs, so a camera look should come before any decision to line.

Signs a sewer line may need more than cleaning

Lining is a repair, not a cleaning, so it only enters the picture once cleaning has stopped working. Repeat clogs in the same month, several drains backing up together and a sewer smell near the cleanout all point at the pipe itself. Wet or sunken ground along the line points the same way.

Florida's Geological Survey adds a local wrinkle. It lists collapsed or broken sewer and drain pipes among the causes of ground sinking that can mimic sinkhole activity. If the ground near your sewer line is dropping, have a licensed plumber camera the pipe before you assume the cause is geology.

What Florida code and Ocoee say about lining

A staff analysis for Florida Building Commission declaratory statement DS 2024-044 says the 2023 Florida Building Code, Plumbing section 718.1 and the residential section P3012.1 call for CIPP rehabilitation to follow ASTM F1216 or F1743. Staff also found that partial lining, often called gapping, and hybrid lining are not covered by those provisions. They are treated as a proposed alternative that needs approval from the local building official.

In plain terms, ask the contractor which standard the liner follows and whether the job is a full or partial lining. A full-length liner built to one of the named ASTM standards sits on firmer ground than a partial one that needs special approval.

The City of Ocoee says plumbing work needs a building permit, applied for at permits.ocoee.org or at the Building Division on the first floor of City Hall. The staff analysis did not discuss permits, and we found no Ocoee rule written specifically for lining a private sewer line. Call the Building Division at 407-905-3104 before the work is scheduled.

One caution on sources. The ICC code notes mentioned above explain the International Plumbing Code, not the Florida code, so treat them as background on how lining works only.
