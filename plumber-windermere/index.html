Wet months

Storm and rain season, June through September and after

Orlando's weather normals put June at about 8 inches of rain, July and August near 7.5 and 7.7, and September about 6.4. That station is Orlando International, not Windermere, but it shows how much of the year's 51 inches arrives in four months. Saturated ground hides slow supply leaks, because a wet yard looks the same as a leaking line. A plumber can check the meter and test the line instead of guessing.

Hurricanes make the wet months sharper. After a storm like that, expect fixtures low in the house to be the first to complain. If a toilet or tub backs up, stop running water and call the emergency line.

Before we arrive: Windermere emergencies and what to do first

These are the situations where you should call the 24/7 line immediately. The steps below are safe to do yourself while dispatch lines up the visit.

Water pouring from a pipe or ceiling

Close the main valve where the line enters the house, then open a low faucet to relieve pressure. Switch off power to wet rooms only if you can reach the panel without stepping in water.

Hot spots or a hissing sound in the floor

A warm patch on a slab floor or a steady hiss with no fixture running can mean a leak in a line under the concrete. Read about slab leak repair and call before the damage spreads.

Sewage rising in a tub or floor drain

Stop all water use, including the washer and dishwasher. Keep children and pets out of the room and do not pour chemical cleaner down the drain.

No water, or a boil water notice

If the whole street is dry, the problem may be on the county side. Orange County Utilities runs an emergency dispatch line around the clock at 407-836-2777, and a boil water hotline at 407-254-9670.

If you smell gas, leave the house and call 911 or your gas utility from outside before you call any plumber. We do not diagnose gas leaks over the phone.

A single dripping faucet or a slow sink is rarely an emergency. Dispatch can often book that for a same-day appointment when available.

Dry months

Dry season: irrigation and the leak your bill reveals

From late fall into spring the rain thins out and sprinklers run more. A cracked irrigation line or a stuck valve can lose water for weeks with no sign above ground. The first clue is often a water bill that jumps with no change in family habits.

That is the time to ask for leak detection. A technician can shut off all fixtures, read the meter, and trace where the loss is. Reclaimed water availability in Windermere is something we could not confirm, so ask the county whether your lot has it before you assume.

Winter cold is rare here but real. The National Weather Service documented record-cold days across central Florida in January 2010. Exposed outdoor pipes and irrigation fittings are the parts most likely to crack in a cold snap, so cover them when a freeze warning is out.
