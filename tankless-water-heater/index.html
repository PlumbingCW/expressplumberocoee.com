Hot water that quits

When the third shower runs cold or the bill keeps climbing

Two people shower, the dishwasher starts, and the next shower turns lukewarm. That is a storage tank running out of hot water, then refilling slowly. The other complaint is the bill, because a tank keeps reheating its stored water all day, even while the house is empty.

A tankless water heater heats water only while a tap is open, using a gas burner or electric elements and no storage tank. In the right Ocoee, FL house it fixes both problems. It also needs planning, because the unit pulls a lot of gas or electricity at once, needs a vent path, and needs a wall to hang on.

It is not unlimited hot water. Every unit has a maximum flow, and two showers plus a washer can still pass it. A technician sizes the unit to your fixtures so that does not happen on a busy morning.

Tank or tankless: the honest trade-offs

Neither type wins in every house. These are the differences that actually change a decision.

Storage tank heater

  • Costs less to buy and install in most homes, and a same-size, same-fuel swap is simple.
  • Delivers hot water until the stored volume is used, then recovers slowly.
  • Keeps heating idle water, so some energy is spent on hours when nobody uses any.
  • Needs floor space and, where a leak would damage the home, a drain pan.

Tankless heater

  • Delivers hot water for as long as demand stays under the unit's flow limit.
  • ENERGY STAR lists a life expectancy of 20 years for tankless units that are maintained.
  • Often costs more to install because of the gas line, venting or electrical work.
  • Hangs on a wall and frees floor space, but needs a clear vent path and clearances.
  • Needs a descale flush on the maker's schedule, and a unit that is never flushed will complain.

Savings follow hot water use, and ENERGY STAR's published figure compares gas tankless with a standard gas tank only. If you only want the same kind of heater back, see our tank water heater replacement page.

Signs your tankless unit needs a flush or descale

A flush means closing the isolation valves, circulating a descaling solution through the heat exchanger with a small pump, then rinsing. The solution and the time come from the manufacturer's manual. A flush cannot repair a failed part, so a technician checks the cause before descaling.

Hot water is weaker or cooler

Scale narrows the passages inside the heat exchanger. Flow drops and the burner or elements cannot heat the thinner stream evenly.

An error code with a beep

Codes differ by brand and can mean scale, a vent problem or a failed sensor. Read the manual, then call. Do not open the cabinet on a gas or electric unit.

Temperature swings mid-shower

Hot, cold, hot again often points to a clogged inlet screen or scaled sensor. Both are checked during service.

Years since the last service

If the unit was never flushed after install, assume it is due. An 18-year-old unit is near ENERGY STAR's 20-year expectation, so ask for a diagnosis before paying to descale.

Water around the unit

Shut off the water to the unit, and the gas or power if you can reach them safely, then call. A flush will not fix a leak.

What drives the cost of a tankless install

NerdWallet's national estimate, updated September 2025, puts tank water heater jobs at $600 to $2,500 and tankless at about $1,400 to $3,900 including installation. NerdWallet also says labor is over half the total. These are national figures, not Ocoee quotes, and your house can land outside them.

Gas line sizeThe Florida Building Code, Fuel Gas, says gas piping must carry enough gas for the combined maximum input of the appliances it serves. A tankless unit has a higher input than a tank, so the line may need upsizing.
VentingA tankless unit needs a vent path that matches the maker's instructions. A short wall run is cheap and a long run through an attic is not.
ElectricalElectric tankless models draw heavy current. The panel and the circuit have to carry it, and that can add real cost.
Removal, fittings and permitThe old tank comes out, the water lines are rerouted to a wall, and isolation valves go in. A permit through the City of Ocoee is part of the job, and water heater installation work follows the same steps.
Credits and rebatesThe federal 25C credit ended for equipment placed in service after December 31, 2025. Duke Energy's water heater rebate excludes tankless. Peoples Gas lists $700 or $550 for gas tankless to its own customers, so check current terms and whether it serves your address.

A homeowners policy usually responds to sudden and accidental damage, such as a heater that bursts and floods a room. It usually does not pay for wear, a slow leak or the heater itself. Ask your insurer before you rely on it.

Ocoee water and a tankless heat exchanger

Ocoee Utilities' 2025 water quality report lists hardness at 5 to 6 grains per gallon. The city's own scale calls that moderate, with soft under 5 and hard over 12. Heat pushes dissolved calcium and magnesium out of water as scale, and a tankless heat exchanger has narrow passages. Plan on flushing on the maker's schedule rather than never.

Moderate is not severe. Many homes at this level manage with regular flushing and no softener. Ask the manufacturer whether it sets a water hardness limit for coverage, and read our water softener and filtration page to see when a softener pays off.

One exception on the city's map is Forest Lake Estates, which Orange County Utilities supplies. The hardness table is not labeled by service area, so a homeowner there should ask that utility for its own report.
