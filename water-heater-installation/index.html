Planning a water heater installation: size, location and fuel

Water heater installation means choosing a unit that fits your household, then connecting its water, fuel or power, venting and safety valves to the manufacturer's instructions. The Florida Building Code, Plumbing, section 502.1 requires exactly that. The plan matters more than the day itself.

Sizing by how you use hot water

Look at the first-hour rating on the label, not only the gallons. It estimates how much hot water the unit can deliver in a busy hour. A household that runs showers back to back needs a different unit than one that spreads use out.

A bigger tank is not always better. It holds more stored heat and takes more floor space.

Moving the heater or switching fuel

Keeping the heater where it is keeps the job simple. Moving it means new piping, and often a new vent, drain or circuit. A switch from gas to a hybrid heat pump unit adds an electrical circuit and an air and drain requirement, and the old gas line must be capped properly.

In new construction and remodels, the location and the gas or power stub are set before the walls close. That belongs with plumbing installation planning. If your old tank has already failed, see water heater replacement instead.

Install day, in order

Confirm the plan

The plumber checks the unit against the label, the space and the fuel supply before touching anything.

Isolate and remove

Water, power and gas are shut off, the old tank is drained safely, and it is disconnected and carried out.

Set and connect

The new unit is leveled, piped and vented. The relief valve gets a proper discharge pipe, and a pan, expansion control or new shutoff valves go in where the setup calls for them.

Fill, test and hand over

The tank is filled before power or flame. Gas joints are leak-tested, and the vent is checked for draft. You get a walk-through of the controls.

Permit and inspection: the Ocoee Building Division

Florida Building Code, Building, section 105.1 requires a permit to install or replace a plumbing, gas or mechanical system. Ocoee takes applications at permits.ocoee.org, and one general application covers all permit types. The Building Division is at 407.905.3104, and its inspector phone window runs weekdays from 7:00 to 8:00 a.m.

Contractors must show active registration, proof of a state license and insurance. Ocoee's forms list no water-heater-specific rule, so ask the Building Division and ask your installer who applies and who schedules the inspection.

Garage, attic or closet in Ocoee homes, and hurricane season

Garage

Garages are easiest to access, but floods reach them first. Ask about raising the unit on a stand. A hybrid unit in a garage draws on that air, which suits it.

Attic

A leak lands on a ceiling, so the pan and its drain matter most. Outdoors, the drain should end 6 to 24 inches above grade. Make sure the access path can take a full tank.

Closet

Check height, combustion air and door type. A closet can suit a gas heater, but starve a hybrid unit of air.

Hurricane readiness

The National Weather Service logged gusts of 55 to 75 mph and 4 to 8 inches of rain in Orange County during Hurricane Milton in 2024, with flooding in low areas. After a flood, a heater whose controls were under water needs a technician's check. When power returns, do not switch an electric heater on until the tank is full.
