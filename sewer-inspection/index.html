The short version

What a camera inspection finds in a sewer line

A sewer camera inspection is a small waterproof camera pushed through your drain line on a flexible rod, so a plumber can watch the pipe's inside on a screen. It turns a guess about a clog into a picture.

On that picture a technician looks for roots entering joints, cracks, pieces that have shifted out of line, low spots where water sits, and a grease coat that narrows the pipe. Each one points to a different fix, and some point to no repair at all. Ask what defect-coding system the plumber uses on the video, such as NASSCO's PACP, because a coded report is easier to compare with a second opinion.

When a camera inspection is worth scheduling

Not every slow drain needs a camera. These situations usually do.

  • ✓You are buying a home and want the sewer line checked before closing.
  • ✓The same drain clogs again within weeks of being cleared.
  • ✓Several fixtures gurgle or back up together.
  • ✓A sewer smell shows up indoors or near the outdoor cleanout.
  • ✓A wet or sunken strip of lawn appears and the irrigation checks out fine.
  • ✓A remodel will add a bathroom, laundry or kitchen to an old line.
  • ✓Roots were cut from the line and you want to see the damage they left.

A smell inside the bathroom is not always the main line. A floor drain or p-trap that has dried out, or one that leaks and cannot hold its water seal, lets gas up too. The camera tells us whether the line is sound, and a plumber then checks the trap separately.

If the line proves clean, simple drain cleaning or a trap repair may be all you need.

The inspection, start to finish

The visit follows the same order every time, so you know what is happening and why.

Find the way in

The camera enters through the outdoor cleanout when there is one. If not, a plumber may pull a toilet or open a vent on the roof to reach the line.

Run the camera

The technician feeds the rod while watching the screen and a distance counter. That counter records how far from the entry point each problem sits.

Mark the spot above ground

A locator tuned to the camera head can pinpoint the position and depth of any defect on the surface. This keeps a later repair small and well aimed.

Review what was seen

You look at the findings with the technician, and a clear price is given before work starts if a repair makes sense. A blocked line must be cleared first, so we may suggest sewer line cleaning before the camera can pass. Real damage leads to sewer line repair.

What a camera can and cannot show

The camera is a strong tool with edges. Knowing them keeps the results from being oversold.

What it can show

  • Roots entering at joints and how thick they are.
  • Cracks, breaks and missing pipe sections.
  • Joints that have shifted out of line.
  • Low spots where water stands.
  • Grease or scale coating the inside of the pipe.
  • How far from the entry point each problem sits.

What it cannot show

  • Anything past a full blockage or a collapsed section.
  • Pressurized supply pipes, which are a separate system.
  • How many more years the pipe will last.
  • Who owns or must repair the stretch that is damaged.
  • A trap or vent problem that sits outside the sewer line.

A clean recording does not rule out every cause of a smell or a slow drain. It rules out the sewer line, which is the expensive suspect, and points the search at branch drains, traps or vents.

Ocoee houses, older and newer, and what to ask the seller

Ocoee has grown in layers, and the age of the street tells you where to look first.

Mostly newer housing

A city housing report built on Census data, dated 2000, says almost all of Ocoee's homes went up after 1960. We found no published list of pipe materials by decade, so the camera, not the street name, shows what your lateral is made of.

Downtown and the oldest homes

The City's own history ties downtown to land platted in 1886, so some homes there are far older than the rest. Older sewer pipe is more likely to have worn joints, which is the main reason to check before buying.

Roots

UF/IFAS says roots grow well wherever moisture and nutrients are available, and roots can reach three to four times the drip line. A mature tree beyond your property line can still reach your lateral, so check the camera footage, not just your own yard.

Sewer or septic

The City's 2002 sewer plan says septic tanks were used for some homes, and the Orange County Health Department permits them. We found no current list of which addresses use septic. Confirm in writing which one your address has, because a septic tank needs a different inspection.

Questions for the seller

Ask whether the line was ever cleared for roots, whether there is a permit record for plumbing work, and where the cleanout is. A seller who cannot answer is a reason to book the camera, and the Building Division at 407-905-3104 can tell you how to ask about permits.
